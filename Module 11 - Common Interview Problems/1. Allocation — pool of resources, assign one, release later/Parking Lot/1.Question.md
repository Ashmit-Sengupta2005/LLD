# Parking Lot — The Question

> **Shape:** Allocation. Same skeleton as BookMyShow, Hotel, Library, Car Rental.

---

## What the interviewer actually says

> *"Design a parking lot system."*

That is the whole question. Sometimes with two hints: *"it has multiple floors"* and *"different vehicle types"*.

Everything else in these notes is **your answer**, not the question. Do not expect a spec.

---

## The 3 questions to ask back (2 minutes, no more)

| Ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Which vehicle types, and does each need its own slot type? | Decides the `VehicleType` enum and the fit rule |
| One floor or many? | Decides whether `Floor` exists as a class |
| Pricing — hourly, flat, or both? | Decides whether `PricingStrategy` is an interface |

Do **not** ask about latency, uptime, or security. That is HLD framing and it wastes your clock.

---

## What you are expected to produce (40 min)

| Part | Time | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clarify | 3 min | the 3 questions above |
| Entities | 5 min | 6 classes, named out loud |
| Entry + exit flow | 2 min | 4 steps each |
| Classes & interfaces | 10 min | written on the board or in the IDE |
| Core methods | 10 min | `park()`, `unpark()`, `calculateFee()` with real bodies |
| Patterns + why | 5 min | 3 patterns, each with a one-line reason |
| Edge cases | 4 min | 2–3 of them |
| Packages | 1 min | one line |

**Ask at the start:** *"Do you want a diagram and class signatures, or working code in an IDE?"* Campus rounds usually want the first, product companies often want the second.

---

## The 2 follow-ups that decide the round ⭐

These come at the end, roughly 90% of the time. Both are **OCP tests**.

**1. "Now add weekend pricing / a monthly pass."**
- Good answer: one new class implementing `PricingStrategy`. Nothing else changes.
- Bad answer: you wrote `if (type == CAR) fee = hours * 20` inside `unpark()`, so you must edit it.

**2. "Now add EV slots with charging."**
- Good answer: one new `VehicleType` value; the factory and the fit rule already handle it.
- Bad answer: a new `if` in three places.

**The sentence to say out loud when you finish:**
> *"Adding a new pricing rule or a new vehicle type costs one new class and no edits to `ParkingLot`."*

---

## Other things they may push on

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |
| "How many `ParkingLot` objects exist?" | One — Singleton. Entry and exit gates share it |
| "Two cars arrive at two gates at the same instant" | Lock the slot allocation. In a real system, an atomic DB update, not a JVM lock |
| "Customer lost the ticket" | Admin override: look up by licence plate, charge a flat penalty |
| "Give the slot nearest the entrance" | That is a second Strategy — `SlotAllocationStrategy` |
| "Where does fee calculation live?" | Service layer, behind `PricingStrategy`. Never in the controller, never in the entity |

---

## Quick Recall

- **Parking Lot = Allocation shape:** pool (`ParkingSlot`) + container (`Floor` → `ParkingLot`) + record (`Ticket`) + actor (`Vehicle`) + **varying rule** (`PricingStrategy`)
- **3 patterns:** Singleton (the lot), Strategy (pricing, allocation), Factory (picking the strategy)
- The whole round is won by making **pricing an interface** before they ask you to change it
- Skip non-functional requirements. Skip 6 services and 5 repositories. You have 40 minutes
